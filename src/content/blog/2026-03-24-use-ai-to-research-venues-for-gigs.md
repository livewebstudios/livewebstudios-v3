Everybody in the band agrees you should be playing more rooms. Nobody wants to spend six weeknights building the list.

So it doesn't get built. The band keeps circling the same four venues, and the calendar looks the same in March as it did in October.

This is the job AI is genuinely good at. Not writing your pitch, not being clever. Just the grinding, hours-long work of finding out what exists within two hours of your house and who books it. An afternoon gets you a target list that would have taken a month of evenings.

## The Research Nobody Wants to Do

Booking research is three boring questions repeated a hundred times. What rooms are there? Do they book live music, and what kind? Who do I talk to?

Each answer lives somewhere different. A venue site, a Facebook events page, a listings calendar, a local paper. Chasing them one at a time is why the list never gets made.

I do this kind of research every week in the web business, mostly to understand a client's competitors before we build anything. The pattern moves over to booking without much change. Ask broadly, then narrow, then verify.

## What to Ask (Example Prompts)

Be specific about geography and about the kind of act you are. A vague prompt gets you a vague list.

Start wide:

> List live music venues within 60 miles of Morristown, New Jersey that regularly book cover bands or tribute acts. For each one, give the venue name, the town, the type of room (bar, club, theater, brewery), and which nights they typically have live music.

Then narrow by what you actually are:

> From that list, which venues book five-piece bands playing classic rock and Motown for a general adult crowd, rather than original acts or singer-songwriters? Note any that have a regular weekend live music night.

Room size matters more than people expect, so ask about it:

> For each venue, estimate the capacity and whether it has a dedicated stage, a PA, and a house sound engineer. Flag any where a band is expected to bring a full PA.

And private work is a separate list:

> List event venues, banquet halls, country clubs, and wedding venues within 60 miles of Morristown, New Jersey that host live bands for weddings and corporate parties. Note whether they keep a preferred vendor list.

Change the town and the mileage and you have the same list for wherever you're willing to drive.

## Turn Results Into a Target List

Get it out of the chat window and into a spreadsheet the same day. A conversation you can't sort is not a list.

Five columns cover it: venue, town, type of room, contact, and notes. Add a sixth for the date you last reached out, because you will absolutely forget.

Then sort by how realistic the room is. Put the ten you could plausibly play this year at the top. A list of ninety venues you'll never contact is the same as no list, just with more guilt attached.

## Verify Before You Pitch

Here's the part nobody should skip. AI gets contact details wrong. Confidently wrong, in full sentences, with a name and an email address that belong to somebody who left in 2019.

So treat every result as a lead, never as a fact. Before you email anyone:

- Open the venue's own website and confirm the booking contact there.
- Check their social page for a recent live music post. A room that stopped booking bands two years ago still shows up in a list.
- Confirm the room still exists. Venues close quietly.
- Never send a pitch to a name you haven't seen on the venue's own page.

Five minutes of checking per venue. It's still a fraction of what the research would have cost you.

## From List to Pitch

Work the top of the list first, in small batches. Five well-researched emails to rooms that actually fit beat forty copy-pasted ones.

Use what the research told you. If a venue has live music every Friday and you noticed they book a lot of horn-section acts, say so in the first line. That one sentence is the difference between a pitch and spam, and it only exists because you did the homework.

Before any of those emails go out, make sure the link in them holds up. A booker clicking through is running the same thirty-second check I wrote about in [what bookers actually look at first](what-venue-bookers-look-at-first.html), and a solid target list won't save a site that [only lives on Facebook](why-a-facebook-page-isnt-a-band-website.html).

Do the research once, properly. Then spend your evenings playing instead of googling.
