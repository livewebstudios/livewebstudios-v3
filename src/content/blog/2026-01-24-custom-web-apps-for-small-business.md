Most of what I build is still websites. That hasn't changed.

But more and more, a client describes a problem and the answer isn't a website at all. It's a tool. So we build those now too.

## What That Actually Means

Software that runs in a browser, on any device, built for how your business specifically works.

Some of what we've built or been asked for:

- A portal where customers log in and see their own information
- Online booking and scheduling
- A quote generator that does the math and produces the document
- An internal dashboard so a team can see the same numbers
- Inventory and product tracking
- Intake forms that go somewhere useful instead of an inbox

None of these are off the shelf. That's the point.

## Why Not Just Buy Software

Sometimes you should, and I'll say so.

If a product does what you need for fifty a month, buy it. I'm not going to build you something worse for more money.

Where custom wins is when you're paying for three products that don't talk to each other, plus somebody's afternoon spent copying between them. Or when the thing you need doesn't exist, so you're bending your business around software written for somebody else's.

## The AI Part

This is what's changed recently, and it's the part I find genuinely interesting.

These tools can now read a document and pull out what matters. Draft the first version of a reply. Sort what comes in and flag what needs a person. Answer the same question you answer forty times a week.

Not magic. Just the boring parts, handled, so the people you pay can do the work you pay them for.

## How It Starts

A conversation about what's eating your week.

Not what software you want. What's annoying. The report that takes an afternoon. The information living in three places. The thing you keep meaning to fix.

That's where these projects actually come from, every time.

Got something like that? Let's talk. Worst case I tell you to go buy something off the shelf, and you've lost twenty minutes.
