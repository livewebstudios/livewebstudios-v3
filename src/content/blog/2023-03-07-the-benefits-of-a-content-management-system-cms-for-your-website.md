A CMS is the admin area you log into to change your own website. Add a page, swap a photo, update your hours, publish a post.

Whether you need one comes down to a single question: how often do you actually want to change something?

## You Don't Need Code

That's the whole point. You log in, you click the page, you type, you save.

No developer, no waiting, no invoice for changing a phone number. For a business that updates things weekly, that's the difference between a site that stays current and one that quietly goes stale.

## It Handles More Than Words

Photos, video, PDFs, galleries. Upload it and drop it on the page.

If you're a contractor posting finished jobs, or a restaurant changing a menu every week, that matters a lot more than it sounds like it does.

## It Grows With You

Start with five pages. Add fifty. Add a blog, a gallery, an events calendar.

You're not rebuilding from scratch every time the business changes shape. That's real value over a few years.

## Now the Honest Part

A CMS is also the thing that breaks.

Every plugin is code from a stranger running on your website. Every one is another update, another possible conflict, another way in for somebody who shouldn't be there. I spent years of Mondays cleaning up after exactly that.

So the security question cuts both ways. Yes, a CMS gets security patches. It also has far more surface to patch.

## How I'd Decide

If you change your site most weeks, get a CMS. The convenience pays for the upkeep.

If you change it twice a year, you're maintaining a machine you barely use. A fast static site and a developer who answers the phone will cost you less and break a lot less often. That's what we build most of the time now, and it's why.
