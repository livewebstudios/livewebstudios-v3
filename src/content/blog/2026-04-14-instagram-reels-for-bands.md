Somebody in the third row already filmed you last Saturday. Fifteen seconds, held up over their head, right as the room started singing.

That clip got seen by about forty people and then disappeared.

It should have been yours. A short vertical clip from the stage is the single cheapest piece of promotion a working band has, and it is the one that reaches people who have never heard of you. You do not have to become a content creator to use it. You have to remember to press record.

## Reels Find New People

Regular posts mostly reach people who already follow you. Short video is different. It gets shown to strangers, and some of those strangers live twenty minutes from the room you are playing next month.

That is the whole reason to bother. A photo keeps your current fans warm. A short clip is how somebody who has never heard your name ends up standing at your show.

And it stacks. A steady trickle of clips tells the platform what your account is about, and it tells a booker scrolling your profile that this band is out working. Two audiences, one habit.

## The Moments Worth Capturing

Not the whole song. The moment inside the song.

What works is anything with people in it. The crowd singing a line back. A room that suddenly fills up between songs. Somebody's hands in the air. Confetti of any kind, including the cheap kind.

What also works, and gets skipped constantly, is the stuff around the music. Load-in on a rainy night. The set list taped to the floor. Two of you laughing at something in the van. People follow bands because bands are a group of humans doing something slightly unreasonable for a living, and the unreasonable part is interesting.

Ask a friend or a partner to film. Not the whole night, just two or three moments. Hand them your phone at the start, tell them what you want, and go play. Trying to film yourself mid-set never works, and trying to remember afterward never works either.

Vertical. Always vertical. A horizontal clip gets cropped into a letterbox and looks like a mistake.

## The First Second Decides

People scroll fast. Faster than feels reasonable.

Whatever is in the first second has to be the best thing in the clip. Not a wide shot of an empty stage, not three seconds of a guitar being plugged in, and definitely not a slow fade from black. Start on the hit. Start on the crowd. Start in the middle.

Which means the moment you filmed almost always needs its front trimmed off. Cut the first two seconds and the same clip works twice as hard.

The other half of this is length. Shorter is better than longer, almost always. If it is good at twenty seconds it is usually better at twelve.

## Text on Screen for Sound-Off Viewers

A lot of people watch with the sound off. Sitting in a waiting room, lying in bed next to somebody, standing in a line. <!-- VERIFY --> If your clip only makes sense with audio, those people scroll past.

So put a line of text on the screen. Instagram will do it for you in a few taps.

Keep it useful. The band's name and the town. What is happening: "Saturday, second set, they knew every word." An upcoming date if there is one. Short enough to read in a glance, high enough on the screen that it is not hidden by the caption.

Three to five hashtags in the caption, chosen on purpose. Your town, your region, the venue, the style of act. A wall of thirty hashtags does not help and it makes the post look desperate.

## A Simple Weekly Habit

Here is the version that survives a real schedule.

<ul>
  <li>One gig night, one person filming, two or three short moments.</li>
  <li>Sunday, pick the best one and trim the front off it.</li>
  <li>Add a line of text, three hashtags, tag the venue, post it.</li>
  <li>Keep the ones you did not use in a folder for a week with no gig.</li>
</ul>

That is maybe fifteen minutes. The bands that do it consistently are not the ones with the most interesting lives. They are just the ones who decided who films and when to post before the night started.

A clip on its own is not a promotion, though. It is one piece. The steady version of this is [the four-post pattern around every show](four-post-pattern-to-promote-a-gig.html), and a good reel slots straight into it as the day-of post or the thank-you.

One more thing, because it comes up every time. Nobody is grading you on how polished it looks. The clip that gets shared is almost never the tidy one. It is the one where a room full of people is clearly having a better night than whoever is watching, and that is a bar you clear just by pointing a phone at the crowd.
