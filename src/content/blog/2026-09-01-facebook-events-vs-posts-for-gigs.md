Eighty-three people marked themselves Interested. Nineteen said they were Going.

Fourteen showed up, and four of those were in the band.

Every working musician has had that night, and it usually produces the same conclusion: Facebook Events do not work. That is the wrong lesson. Events and regular posts do two different jobs, and most bands are asking one of them to do both.

## Two Tools, Two Jobs

A regular post is broadcast. It goes into feeds, it can reach people who do not follow you, and it carries energy. Its job is attention.

An Event is a container. It holds the details, it sits in somebody's calendar, and it can remind them on the day. Its job is memory.

Attention without memory is somebody who liked your post three weeks ago and has genuinely no idea you are playing tonight. Memory without attention is a perfectly organized Event that four people ever saw.

You need both, and you need them doing the thing each is good at.

## What an Event Does Well

Three things, and they are worth having.

It holds the details in one place that stays correct. Address, start time, cover, age policy, parking. When somebody asks "what time do you start," you send a link instead of typing it again.

It reminds people. Anybody who marked themselves Going or Interested can get a nudge as the date approaches, which is the single most useful thing the format does. Most bands never use it, and it is right there.

And it gives you something to post into. A week out, post an update inside the Event with the set you are excited about or a clip from last time. Everybody attached to it hears from you again without you burning a slot in the main feed.

What an Event does badly is reach. On its own it sits there quietly, which is why the band that only makes an Event wonders where everybody was.

## What a Post Does Well

Posts are where the people are.

A post lands in feeds, gets shared, gets commented on, and can be seen by friends of people who engaged with it. That is how somebody who has never heard of you finds out about Saturday.

Posts also carry tone. A photo of the room from last time, a clip that made everybody laugh, a line about the song you finally added. None of that fits inside an Event's details field, and all of it is what makes a person decide to come rather than just note that a thing is happening.

So posts do the persuading and the Event does the remembering. Link the Event from every post, so anybody you have just persuaded has somewhere to put it.

The four posts around every show are the engine here, and [that pattern](four-post-pattern-to-promote-a-gig.html) works the same whether or not an Event exists. The Event just gives each of those posts a destination.

## Co-Host With the Venue

This is the highest-value thing in this whole article and it takes one message.

Ask the venue to co-host the Event. When they accept, the Event appears on their page too, and it can reach their followers, who are people who already come to that room to see live music.

<ul>
  <li>Create the Event yourself, so you control the details.</li>
  <li>Invite the venue's page as a co-host when you set it up.</li>
  <li>Ask the other bands on the bill as well. Everybody's audience, one Event.</li>
  <li>Send the request early. Nobody accepts a co-host invite at four on the afternoon of the show.</li>
</ul>

Some venues run their own Event and would rather you share theirs. That is fine, and it is better than two competing Events for the same night, which splits everybody and confuses people about which one is real.

Ask once, politely, when the gig is confirmed. It becomes routine after the first time, and venues notice bands who do it. That is the same currency [tagging the room in every post](cross-promotion-for-bands.html) earns you.

## The Mistakes That Sink Both

A short list, and most bands make at least two.

Treating Interested as a headcount. It is not. It is closer to a bookmark than a commitment, and planning your night around it leads to disappointment. Use it as a signal about which shows have momentum, not as an attendance figure.

Making the Event and then never touching it. An Event with no posts in it and no updates is a flyer taped to a wall in an empty hallway.

Creating the Event four days out. People need to see a date while their calendar is still open. A few weeks ahead is the window.

Leaving stale details up. A start time that changed, a cover charge that is wrong, a venue address that has a typo. People arriving confused is worse than people not arriving.

And posting only the Event link with no words around it. A bare link is the least shareable thing you can put in a feed.

Get those five right and the format stops being a disappointment. Post to reach people, Event to hold them, venue on the co-host line, and something worth reading in both.
