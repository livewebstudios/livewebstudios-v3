AI made building a website faster. It didn't make it free, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling a template with a new label on it.

So what does an AI-built website actually cost in 2026? The honest answer is that it depends on a short list of things, and you can figure out most of them before you ever talk to anyone. This article walks through what drives the number, what AI genuinely made cheaper, what it didn't, and the questions worth asking any studio or agency before you sign.

## What AI actually made cheaper

Start with the labor. Writing the HTML and CSS by hand used to take real hours. So did drafting page copy, writing alt text for every image, building schema markup, producing ten versions of a service-area page. That's the work AI now does in a fraction of the time.

In our studio, AI writes the code and the first draft of the copy. It checks the markup, flags broken links and runs through a pre-launch checklist. Across seven months that system handled 14 full site rebuilds for very different businesses, from a church to a power washing company.

That's where the savings come from. Drafting got fast. Revisions got fast. A change that used to mean waiting a week can come back the same day.

## What AI didn't make cheaper

Judgment. Somebody still has to decide what the site is for, who it's talking to and what the visitor should do next. Somebody has to read every page before it goes live and catch the wrong phone number, the service you stopped offering, the sentence that sounds nothing like you.

That review is the difference between an AI-built site and an AI-generated one. The first has a person accountable for every line. The second is whatever the software produced. They can look similar on launch day. They don't age the same way.

In an AI-staffed studio, a human principal directs the work and signs off on every output. That time costs money. It should. It's the part you're actually paying for.

## What drives the price

These are the things that move the number, roughly in order of how much.

### How many pages, and how different they are

A five-page site for a local service business is a different project from a forty-page site with a page for every service in every town. Similar pages are cheap for AI to produce. Genuinely different pages, each with its own purpose, take more direction and more review.

### Who supplies the content

If you hand over finished copy and good photos, the job gets simpler. If the studio writes everything from a phone interview, that's more work. AI helps a lot here, but someone still has to get your story right, and that takes a real conversation.

### Moving from an old site

A brand-new business with no site is the simplest case. Moving an existing site is more involved. Every old URL that ranks needs a redirect so you don't lose your Google standing. Content has to be pulled out and cleaned up. If the old site was hacked, there's extra cleanup work. We walked through that process in [How We Rebuilt a Hacked WordPress Site Without WordPress](hacked-wordpress-to-ai-rebuild.html).

### Features beyond pages

A contact form is simple. Online booking, a store, a members area or a connection to your CRM each add real work. Some of those push a project off a static site entirely, and a good studio will tell you when that's the case.

### Whether you'll edit it yourself

If you want to post your own blog or update your own hours, the site needs a content editor set up and a little training. We use Decap CMS for that. It's light, but it's still part of the build.

### Media

Stock photos are cheap. Custom photography costs more. Generative media, like a hero film or edited composites, sits somewhere in between depending on how much direction it needs.

## The ongoing cost people forget

The build is one number. What it costs to keep the site running is the other.

A traditional WordPress site carries ongoing overhead: plugin updates, security patches, a database, often a premium theme license and a page-builder subscription. Something always needs attention. That time adds up, whether you pay someone for it or lose your own evenings to it.

A static, AI-built site cuts most of that out. No plugins, no database, nothing to patch on a Tuesday night. Hosting on a platform like Netlify is typically cheaper than traditional managed hosting. The files are yours. There's no monthly platform fee just to keep existing.

Ask any provider what the site costs to run in year two. The answer tells you a lot.

## Red flags

A few things should make you slow down.

- **A price with no questions asked.** If someone quotes you before they know what your business does, you're buying a template.
- **You don't own the files.** Some platforms lease you a site. Leave and it's gone.
- **No human review.** Ask who reads the pages before launch. If the answer is "the AI checks it," walk away.
- **Nobody mentions redirects.** On a site move, that's how rankings disappear.

## Questions to ask before you sign

Bring these to any conversation, with us or anyone else:

1. Who decides the structure of the site, and who reads every page before it goes live?
2. Do I own the code and the content outright?
3. What happens to my current URLs and rankings?
4. Can I make edits on my own, and what does that involve?
5. What does it cost to keep the site running after launch?
6. What's included in revisions, and how fast do they come back?
7. If something breaks, who do I call, and do they pick up?

A good provider will answer all seven without squirming.
