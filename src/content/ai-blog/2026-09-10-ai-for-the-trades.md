The trades make up a big share of the businesses we talk to, and the question they bring has changed. It used to be "do I need a website?" Now it's "what am I supposed to do about AI?"

Here's our honest answer. A plumber doesn't need most of what gets sold as AI. A plumber needs the phone to ring, the estimate to go out the same day, and the invoice to get paid. AI is useful exactly where it helps with those three things. Everywhere else, it's noise.

## Start with the website, because that's where the calls come from

When a pipe bursts at 11pm, nobody reads your About page. They pull out a phone, search, and tap the first number that looks legit. That's the whole funnel.

So the site has to load fast on a phone, show the number up top, make it tappable, and say clearly which towns you cover. It needs your license info and real photos of real work. That's it. It doesn't need a chatbot pretending to be a dispatcher.

This is where AI already does heavy lifting for us. We build trade sites as static HTML, written by AI and reviewed line by line by a person. No WordPress, no plugins, nothing for a bot to break into. D&J Power Washing is one of the 14 sites we've rebuilt this way. The pages are small and quick, and the owner never gets an email about a plugin update.

AI also handles the parts of a site build that used to get skipped because they were tedious. Service-area pages written for each town. Schema markup so Google knows you're a plumber in a specific county and not a blog about plumbing. Alt text on every photo. Nobody enjoys writing that stuff. Now nobody has to.

## Follow-up is where the money leaks

Most trades businesses don't have a marketing problem. They have a follow-up problem.

The lead comes in through the site form while you're under a sink. By the time you see it at 7pm, they've called somebody else. Or the estimate sits in a notebook in the truck for three days. Or the job gets done and the invoice goes out two weeks later, and then it takes another month to get paid.

This is the work AI is actually good at. Not the creative stuff. The boring, repeatable, "this should have happened already" stuff.

- **Form triage.** A new inquiry gets sorted, tagged by job type, and a reply drafted in your words, waiting for you to approve with one tap.
- **Estimate reminders.** An estimate that hasn't been answered in a few days gets a polite nudge, drafted and queued.
- **Invoices that go out on schedule.** We run our own hosting-renewal invoices this way, on Zoho and Google Apps Script. The system knows who's due and when. We wrote it up in [The Invoice That Sends Itself](the-invoice-that-sends-itself.html).

Notice the pattern. AI drafts. You approve. Nothing goes to a customer without a human deciding it should.

## Keep a human on the phone

This is where we'll disagree with a lot of AI vendors.

There's a big push right now to put AI voice agents on the phones of small service businesses. For some businesses, maybe. For a plumber, we think it's usually a mistake. People calling a tradesperson are often stressed, sometimes standing in an inch of water. They want to hear a person who knows what a shutoff valve is.

Use AI behind the scenes so you have more time to answer the phone yourself. Don't use it to avoid answering. We go deeper on this in [Automating Your Front Office Without Losing Your Voice](front-office-automation-your-voice.html).

## Reviews and social, without the time sink

Two more places AI pulls real weight for the trades.

Reviews first. Every Google review deserves a reply, and most owners never get to them. AI can draft a specific, human-sounding reply to each one. You read it, fix anything off, post it. A few minutes a week instead of never.

Then social. Most trade businesses post a photo once, feel guilty for six months, then post again. A simple posting automation can take the job photos you already snap on your phone and turn them into a steady schedule on Facebook and Instagram. We run Facebook and Instagram posting automation in the studio. It doesn't need to be clever. It needs to be consistent.

## What you can skip

A lot of what gets pitched to contractors right now is solving problems you don't have.

You probably don't need an AI chatbot on your site. You probably don't need AI-generated blog posts about "10 signs your water heater is failing" cranked out by the dozen. Thin content like that doesn't help you rank and doesn't make the phone ring. And you definitely don't need a monthly subscription to a platform you'll log into twice.

If a tool can't point to a call, an estimate or a payment it helped produce, pass on it.

## Where to start

If you run a trade business and want to try this without blowing up your week, pick one thing.

Usually it's invoicing, because the payoff is immediate and the rules are clear. Sometimes it's the website, if yours is slow or hard to use on a phone. Occasionally it's review replies, because that one costs nothing to test.

Whatever you pick, set it up so a person approves the output until you trust it. That's how we run every system in the studio, and it's why the systems hold up.
