There are two very different things for sale under the label "AI help." One is advice. The other is a working system. Both are useful. They are not the same purchase, and small businesses get burned when they buy one expecting the other.

Here's how to tell them apart, and how to figure out which one you actually need.

## What an AI consultant does

A consultant looks at your business and tells you where AI fits. That might be a workflow assessment, a tool recommendation, a policy on what staff can and can't paste into a chatbot, or a training session. Good ones are worth every dollar. They save you from buying the wrong software and from the expensive mistake of automating a broken process.

The deliverable is usually a document. A roadmap, a report, a slide deck. Then the consultant steps back and somebody on your side does the building.

That last sentence is the whole question. **Who is going to build it?**

If you have an office manager who loves spreadsheets, an IT person or a vendor already on call, a consultant can be exactly right. You get the plan. Your people execute.

## What a studio that runs on AI does

A studio that runs on AI does the work itself, and it already runs its own business on the same systems it sells.

That second part matters. We don't just recommend automation. Our invoices, reports, content calendars and site builds run through AI-staffed systems every day, directed by a human principal who signs off on every output. When we tell a client that a monthly report can build itself, it's because ours does. When we say a newsletter engine works, we're describing something we use.

The deliverable isn't a document. It's a running system. The invoice that sends itself. The report that shows up on the first of the month. The website that's live and fast. ## The honest overlap

The line isn't perfectly clean. We do consulting too: workflow assessments, and a legal AI consulting engagement where the job was advice and policy, not a build. Some consultants also build.

So don't hire based on the label. Hire based on what you'll have when the engagement ends.

- **A plan and a team that will execute it.** A consultant is probably the right call.
- **A plan and nobody to execute it.** You'll end up paying twice. Once for the plan, once for someone to build it.
- **No plan, and no appetite to manage a build.** You want someone who can assess and build, and then keep it running.

## Questions to ask either one

Whoever you talk to, these questions sort people out fast.

### "What have you actually shipped?"

Not "what do you know about." What's running right now, for a real business? Ask to see it. A consultant should be able to point to recommendations that got implemented. A studio should be able to show you live work. We keep ours on a proof page for exactly this reason.

### "Do you use this in your own business?"

It's a simple question and it tells you a lot. Someone who runs their own operations on AI has already hit the rough edges. Someone who only advises on it may be working from demos.

### "Who checks the output?"

AI makes confident mistakes. Any serious provider has a review step, and they should be able to tell you exactly who does it and when. If the answer is "the AI checks itself," keep looking.

### "Who owns the accounts and the code when we're done?"

You should. Your domain, your automation scripts, your logins. If a provider builds everything inside their own accounts, you're renting, and leaving gets expensive.

### "What happens when it breaks?"

Automations break. An API changes, a password expires, a form field gets renamed. Ask who notices, who fixes it, and how fast. A plan with no support attached is a plan to be stuck.

### "How do you handle confidential information?"

For law firms, medical offices and anyone with client files, this comes first. What tools touch your data? Where is it stored? What is never sent to an AI model at all? A vague answer here is a no.

## Red flags on either side

A few things should make you walk.

**Big promises with no specifics.** "We'll transform your operations" means nothing. "We'll automate your renewal invoices in Zoho" means something.

**No review step.** Anyone who says the AI handles everything unsupervised is either overselling or not checking.

**Pricing before discovery.** Nobody can quote a real automation without looking at your actual workflow first.

**Invented results.** Testimonials with no names, case studies with no details. If it can't be checked, don't count it.

## So which one?

If you've got the people to build and just need direction, hire an independent consultant, and a good one will pay for itself.

If you want the thing built and running, and you'd rather not manage a project on top of running your business, hire a studio that already runs on what it sells. Ask to see the proof. Then ask them the six questions above anyway.
