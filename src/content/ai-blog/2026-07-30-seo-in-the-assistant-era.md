A customer used to type "plumber near me" into Google and scroll. Now a lot of them ask an AI assistant instead, and they get one answer back, sometimes with two or three businesses named in it. If yours isn't one of them, you never had a chance.

That shift has a lot of business owners worried their SEO is suddenly worthless. It isn't. But some of it matters more now, and some of it matters less.

## What didn't change

AI assistants don't invent their answers from nothing. They read the web. When an assistant recommends a business, it's pulling from the same places Google does: your website, your Google Business Profile, your reviews, directory listings, and anything else written about you.

So the fundamentals still hold.

- **A fast, clean website.** Slow pages and broken markup hurt you with search engines and with the systems that feed AI answers.
- **Clear pages for each service.** One page that says exactly what you do, where, and for whom beats a single page that lists everything.
- **Consistent business details.** Same name, address, and phone number everywhere. Mismatches make you look like two different businesses, or like nobody.
- **Real reviews.** Assistants lean hard on them.

If your Google ranking is weak, your odds of showing up in AI answers are weak too. The two aren't separate projects.

## What changed

### Answers beat links

Search results give a customer a list to choose from. An assistant hands them a short answer. That means the page that best answers the question plainly gets quoted, and a page stuffed with keywords around a vague sales pitch gets skipped.

Write like you're answering a customer on the phone. "Yes, we service tankless water heaters. We work on most major brands and we cover Bergen and Passaic counties." That sentence is easy for a person to read and easy for a machine to repeat.

### Structure matters more

This is where schema comes in. Schema is a small block of code on your page that tells machines, in their own format, what the page is: a local business, a service, an FAQ, an article. People never see it. Search engines and AI systems read it directly.

We do a lot of schema work, and it's one of the most overlooked jobs on small business sites. Plenty of the sites we audit either have none or have a plugin-generated block that's wrong. When the machine-readable version of your business is accurate, you're much easier to recommend.

### Being mentioned counts

Assistants weigh what other people say about you, not only what you say about yourself. Local news, industry directories, chamber listings, a supplier's "where to buy" page. Each one is another source confirming you exist and do what you say.

## What you can check this week

You don't need us for these. Honestly, start here.

### Ask the assistants

Open two or three AI assistants and ask the questions your customers ask. "Best roofer in Hackensack." "Who does kitchen remodels in Paramus." See who gets named. If it's your competitors, look at what their sites and profiles have that yours doesn't.

### Claim your Google Business Profile

If it isn't claimed and filled in completely, do it today. Hours, services, service area, photos, a real description. It's the single most-read source about a local business.

### Fix your contact details

Search your business name and check every listing on the first two pages. Anything outdated, fix it or request the fix.

### Read your own service pages

Does each one answer the obvious question in the first two sentences? If a customer has to scroll to find out whether you do the thing, rewrite it.

## What to be skeptical of

Someone will offer to get you "ranked in ChatGPT" for a monthly fee. Be careful. Nobody controls what assistants say, and anyone who guarantees a spot is guessing or lying. What you can do is make your business easy to find, easy to understand, and well described across the web. That's the whole game, and it's the same game it's always been. The scoreboard just moved.
